1. Course Overview

This course introduces the major theories, concepts, and analytical tools of international marketing and examines how they are applied to create value across borders. Topics include market research, consumer behavior, market selection, branding, product and service strategy, pricing, integrated communication, distribution, cross-border e-commerce, and sustainable global marketing.

The 2026 course integrates international marketing theory, Chinese business practice, data analytics, and AI-agent-supported workflows. Students will analyze real international business cases, with particular attention to Chinese enterprises, Shenzhen-based firms, and the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area. In teams, students will design and evaluate the launch of a product, service, or brand in a new international market.

2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

  1. Explain the role and process of marketing in domestic and international contexts.
  2. Assess international market opportunities using cultural, economic, political, legal, and competitive evidence.
  3. Develop market-entry, segmentation, positioning, product, pricing, communication, and channel strategies.
  4. Compare Chinese firms' globalization with foreign brands' localization in China.
  5. Use data analytic tools to collect, clean, visualize, and interpret international marketing data.
  6. Design and supervise an AI agent workflow for research, monitoring, analysis, or campaign support, and critically verify its outputs.
  7. Evaluate cross-border e-commerce channels and select an appropriate channel portfolio for a target market.
  8. Communicate evidence-based recommendations clearly in English or Chinese while following academic integrity, privacy, and responsible-AI standards.

6. Final Project Requirements

Teams will select a new brand, product, or service from China for entry into another country or region, or a foreign offering for entry into China. The final submission must develop and evaluate an innovative international marketing strategy.

  1. Define the target market, research question, customer segment, and strategic objective.
  2. Collect credible consumer, category, competitor, platform, and/or campaign data and document the sources.
  3. Use at least one data analytic tool to clean, analyze, and visualize the evidence. Include reproducible calculations or an analysis appendix.
  4. Build and use at least one AI agent workflow for a meaningful project task such as structured market research, competitor monitoring, data preparation, scenario analysis, or campaign support.
  5. Submit an AI-use appendix containing the tool and model used, workflow or prompts, inputs, selected outputs, verification process, corrections, limitations, and human decisions.
  6. Recommend a cross-border e-commerce channel portfolio. Compare relevant options such as global marketplaces, regional/local marketplaces, direct-to-consumer websites, social commerce, B2B platforms, distributors/agents, and omnichannel partners.
  7. Address channel economics and execution: customer acquisition, commissions, payments, logistics and fulfillment, returns, localization, data access, platform dependence, and compliance.
  8. Present findings orally and submit an essay of at least 5,000 English words (or an instructor-approved equivalent), supported by figures, tables, charts, and properly cited sources.

9. Academic Integrity and Submission Standards

  1. Plagiarism, fabricated citations, invented data, and unverified AI-generated evidence are prohibited.
  2. Cite all ideas, data, reports, images, tables, and other sources using a consistent academic citation style.
  3. Label original analysis separately from external evidence and retain source files, calculations, prompts/workflows, and verification notes.
  4. Both English and Chinese are accepted for oral presentation unless the instructor specifies otherwise; submitted writing should follow the announced language requirement.
