Lecture outline

Part 1Data types, levels of measurement and structures: numerical vs categorical, cross-section / time-series / panel, structured, semi-structured and unstructured data, complex and high-dimensional types (text, image, audio, video, graph, spatio-temporal)
Part 2Existing sources: in-house proprietary data, open data, third-party (3P) and alternative data; evaluating a source along coverage, quality, cost, compliance and reproducibility
Part 3Acquiring data: REST APIs (authentication, pagination, rate limits, caching) and web scraping, with the legal and ethical boundaries
Part 4Primary collection: surveys, sampling, interviews, card sorting, laboratory and online behavioural experiments, A/B tests, nonresponse bias and labelling consistency
Part 5Quality, compliance and 2026 practice: data contracts and data products, open table formats, vector databases and embeddings, synthetic data, privacy-enhancing technologies, PIPL/GDPR and copyright
Part 6Pipelines: from sources to analytic tables, ET vs ELT, SQL, caching and reproducible acquisition scripts
Part 7Workflow: the data-source inventory, the acquisition script, documentation, and this week's deliverable

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish data types, structures (cross-section / time-series / panel) and levels of measurement, and choose methods accordingly;
  • Evaluate a data source along coverage, quality, cost, compliance and reproducibility;
  • Explain the difference and use cases of in-house proprietary, open, third-party (3P) data, alternative data and synthetic data;
  • Acquire data properly with APIs (REST / auth / pagination / rate limits / caching) and web scraping, while respecting legal and ethical boundaries;
  • Design surveys, sampling and A/B experiments for primary collection, handling nonresponse bias and labelling consistency;
  • Deliver a reproducible data-source inventory plus acquisition script, declaring any AI tools you used.
