Lecture outline

Part 1 · 6–24Foundations: learning from examples rather than rules, why data/compute/algorithms changed the constraint, the ML family tree, supervised versus unsupervised tasks, deep and reinforcement learning, ML in finance and in marketing, and the contrast with econometrics (prediction versus causal inference)
Part 2 · 25–35Evaluating performance: generalisation, overfitting and underfitting, the bias–variance–base-error decomposition, the three-way data partition, complexity and learning curves, Occam's razor and penalisation, sampling bias, cross-validation
Part 3 · 36–43Supervised learning: linear and logistic baselines, CART, ensembles and random forests, k-NN and SVM, a comparison table for choosing among learners, and reading a classifier beyond accuracy (precision, recall, calibration)
Part 4 · 44–49Unsupervised learning: weak, semi- and self-supervision, PCA, k-means, hierarchical clustering and dendrograms, anomaly detection, and how to judge a segmentation
Part 5 · 50–57Networks and reinforcement: network anatomy, what a node computes, activation functions, forward propagation, backpropagation, what depth buys, the RL loop, and brand associations as a network
Part 6 · 58–62Practice: a checklist for reading an ML result, a minimal scikit-learn workflow, the common pitfalls (leakage, imbalance, drift), and a summary

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish supervised, unsupervised, deep and reinforcement learning, and match each paradigm to the problem it fits;
  • Explain generalisation, overfitting and underfitting, and the bias–variance trade-off that produces them;
  • Describe the main supervised learners — penalised regression, SVM, k-NN, CART, ensembles and random forests — and select among them with a validation set;
  • Describe the main unsupervised methods — PCA, k-means, hierarchical clustering, anomaly detection — and judge a segmentation by its usefulness, not its tidiness;
  • Explain what a neural network computes, what depth adds, and how reinforcement learning differs from supervised learning;
  • Read an ML result critically: label definition, split discipline, baseline, metric and threshold, stability, and what happens when the world moves.

Key distinction carried through the lecture

A predictive model answers what will happen; a causal model answers what happens if we intervene. The lecture keeps both in view: out-of-sample error is the criterion for prediction, but it cannot identify an effect — that argument returns in Week 7 and in the project's identification section.
