Lecture outline

Part 0Cover and course information: learning objectives, roadmap, course guideline, assessment and the AI-use policy
Part 1Foundations of big data and analytics: DIKW, the 5 V, data modalities, the data pipeline, the modern data stack, levels of analytics, CRISP-DM versus MLOps/LLMOps
Part 2AI and machine learning basics: the AI landscape, machine learning 1-2-3, learning paradigms, bias–variance, evaluation metrics (including LLM evaluation), representation learning, deep learning, Transformers, post-training alignment, reasoning models, agentic AI and MCP, small and on-device models, RAG
Part 3AI advances 2022–2026: timeline, model comparison, capability and cost curves, compute and capital expenditure, data centres and power, data and copyright, the evaluation crisis, hallucination and mitigation, EU and China regulation, enterprise adoption and ROI, labour-market evidence
Part 4Tools and stack: the analytics toolbox, languages compared, visualisation and BI, data engineering and cloud, ML/DL libraries, the LLM application stack, reproducibility, cost and compute choices
Part 5Data exploration and modelling: the end-to-end workflow, EDA, feature engineering, text exploration, the modelling ladder, cross-validation, model interpretation, deployment and monitoring, evaluation and guardrails
Part 6–7Preview of the case map, course map, discussion questions, takeaways, reading list, the assignment and the AI declaration, next-lecture preview

Learning objectives

  • Describe the big-data ecosystem, the data pipeline and the modern data stack, and set up a reproducible analysis environment;
  • Frame any analytics task with the ``machine learning 1-2-3'' template (data → model → optimisation → evaluation);
  • Explain the basic principles and the limits of foundation models, reasoning models, agentic AI and RAG;
  • Recognise the risks of generative AI: hallucination, evaluation reliability, cost and data compliance;
  • Use AI tools under the course policy (declare tools and prompts, verify output) and complete a first data-exploration task.
