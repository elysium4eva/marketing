AI Foundation — Supplementary Lecture 1: Understanding LLMs and AI Agents

About this series. These two supplementary lectures give students with no AI background a working, non-technical understanding of large language models and AI agents, so that the AI-agent workflows used in the course project can be followed — and responsibly evaluated — rather than treated as a black box. Content is adapted and translated from the instructor's Intelligent Trade course and re-badged for International Marketing Strategy.

Chapter outline

Part 1AI: Making Machines Solve Problems — where artificial intelligence came from, the Turing test, symbolic AI and its limits
Part 2From Writing Rules to Learning from Data — how machine learning emerged, learning functions from data, training and generalisation
Part 3Neural Networks: Machines that Learn Features — from the perceptron to multi-layer networks, why depth matters
Part 4More Layers, More Data, More Compute — the deep learning breakthrough, GPUs, large datasets and representation learning
Part 5LLMs: Giant Networks Predicting the Next Token — how large language models generate text, attention, pre-training and fine-tuning
Part 6Prompts, Knowledge Bases and Enterprise Data — from models to business tools, prompt engineering, RAG and retrieval
Part 7Models Judge; Agents Get Things Done — from LLMs to AI agents, planning, tool calling and memory
Part 8From Single AI Tools to Full Business Loops — tool combinations in trade and marketing tasks, worked end-to-end workflows
Part 9Use, Compare, and Take Responsibility — risks, hallucination, evaluation and class practice
Part 10Summary — from learning functions to completing tasks

Learning objectives

  • Explain, in plain language, where AI came from and why machine learning replaced hand-written rules;
  • Describe how neural networks and deep learning learn features from data, and why scale changed everything after 2012;
  • Follow how a large language model generates text (next-token prediction, attention) and what pre-training and fine-tuning do;
  • Distinguish a model from a business tool: prompts, knowledge bases, RAG and enterprise data;
  • Explain what turns an LLM into an AI agent — planning, tool calling, memory — and how single AI tools combine into full business loops;
  • Apply the course's responsible-use rules: verify agent outputs, disclose AI use, and take responsibility for evidence.
