Chapter outline

Part 1Documentation from day one: the four settings, the documentation acceptance criteria, the 20-item checklist, metadata standards and an automatic data dictionary
Part 2Univariate statistics: central tendency, dispersion and distribution shape, robustness, kurtosis, univariate pitfalls
Part 3Bivariate and multivariate: correlation, contingency tables, conditional distributions and Simpson's paradox, restricted range
Part 4The grammar of graphics: data → geometry → channel, colour palettes, choosing a chart in five steps, self-contained captions and figure/table conventions
Part 5Tools, maps and interaction: the Python stack, Stata and R compared, interactive plotly charts, dashboards, choropleth maps and classification methods, Moran's I and spatial autocorrelation
Part 6Honest graphics: the four misleading-chart families (restricted range, area and 3D illusions, dual axes and cherry-picking, statistics meets graphics), colour-blind friendly and accessible design
Part 7–8High dimensions: projections (PCA, t-SNE, Isomap), parallel coordinates, clustering and interpretability; project: the report pipeline and the one-page visual report

Learning objectives

  • Write the data documentation and metadata at the very start of a project (including the 20-item checklist and a field dictionary);
  • Describe a variable completely with the trio of central tendency, dispersion and distribution shape, and state how robust each of them is;
  • Use correlation, contingency tables and conditional distributions correctly in bivariate and multivariate settings, and recognise the effect of a restricted range;
  • Choose a chart from the grammar of graphics and a decision table, and write self-contained captions;
  • Produce interactive charts, dashboards and geo-visualisations in Python (choropleth, symbol map, spatial autocorrelation);
  • Recognise and fix misleading charts, build colour-blind friendly, accessible graphics, and report conclusions with a narrative structure.
