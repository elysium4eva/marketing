AI Foundation — Supplementary Lecture 2: AI Tools and the Performance of Large Models

About this series. These two supplementary lectures give students with no AI background a working, non-technical understanding of large language models and AI agents, so that the AI-agent workflows used in the course project can be followed — and responsibly evaluated — rather than treated as a black box. This second lecture covers the large-model landscape, the agent tool stack and third-party evaluation: how to read capability, cost and risk, and how to choose a model with an auditable process instead of a headline. Content is adapted and translated from the instructor's Intelligent Trade course and re-badged for International Marketing Strategy.

Learning objectives

  • Frame model comparison around capability, cost and risk together, instead of a single leaderboard rank;
  • Map the industry's five-layer stack — frontier labs, China's open-weights camp, Europe, and the ecosystem layer that decides how models are used;
  • Retell the three threads of modern model history: unified attention, scaling laws, and alignment;
  • Explain how agents moved from talking to working (ReAct → function calling → multi-agent → MCP) and what the tool-calling loop looks like;
  • Classify any model along seven dimensions and match it to a task's modality, budget, latency, compliance and deployment constraints;
  • Read third-party leaderboards critically: know who evaluates what, spot contamination and configuration traps, and convert per-token prices into per-task costs;
  • Run an auditable selection process — scorecard, proof of concept, and written rationale — suitable for documentation in the course project's AI-use appendix.
